How the peg pyramid works

The board is built like a triangle of staggered pegs. Each row is one peg wider than the row above it, so the ball has a fresh left-or-right choice at every level. By the time it reaches the bottom, it has followed a chain of simple random turns and lands in one of the pockets below.

Because the paths are driven by repeated fifty-fifty deflections, most balls settle near the center while fewer reach the far edges. Mathematically, this is the same logic behind a Galton board and a binomial curve. The payout table simply flips that curve so the common pockets pay less and the rare edge pockets pay more.

Multipliers, odds, and the house margin

Each pocket value reflects the chance of landing there, minus a small house margin. If you multiply the payout by the probability of reaching that pocket, the total comes in slightly below one. That difference is the operator’s edge. Most published versions of the game list return values in the 97% to 99% range, depending on the provider and settings.

Six words worth knowing

Pocket
A slot at the bottom of the board that holds the multiplier for the finished ball.
Rows
The number of peg lines the ball crosses; more rows mean more pockets and tighter odds.
Risk level
A preset that changes how the payout table is spread without shifting the house edge much.
House margin
The small gap between true odds and paid odds that supports the operator.
Provably fair
A system using published hashes so a player can check that the result was not changed.
Variance
The amount of swing around the average result, which is what the risk switch really adjusts.

Straight answers to five questions

Is every drop random?

On licensed platforms, yes. Each movement comes from an audited random number generator, and provably fair versions let you verify the final path after the round is done.

Do more rows improve payouts?

No. Extra rows add more pockets and stretch the odds curve, so edge payouts become larger while their chance gets smaller. The return stays broadly the same.

Which risk setting is best?

There is no mathematically best setting. Low risk keeps the ride steadier, while high risk concentrates value in the outer pockets. Choose the one that fits your budget and temperament.

Can I practice without using money?

Yes. The board on this page runs with virtual credits, and many operators keep a free version with the same maths. The payout logic does not change when real money is added.

Does timing affect where the ball lands?

No. The result is set by the random generator, not by how quickly or slowly you press the button. Any pattern you see is usually just coincidence.
